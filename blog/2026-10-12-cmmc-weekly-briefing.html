CMMC Weekly Briefing · Oct 5–9, 2026

Quiet week on CMMC rules as task force report stays under wraps

Published October 12, 2026 · Compiled by Vigilant Cybersecurity

Top story

It was a quiet week. We found no new DoW CIO memo, class deviation, Federal Register action, or DOJ cyber-fraud settlement dated October 5–9. The CMMC Reform Task Force report is still not public. At the Cyber AB's September town hall, CEO Matt Travis gave a personal estimate of release in "the back half of October at the earliest," after reviews outside the task force, and said he had no confirmed timeline. The most substantive item of the week was CMMC.com's October 8 breakdown of Redspin's annual defense industrial base survey. It shows most contractors did not stop working toward certification when Phase 2 was paused.

Rules and policy

  • Revision 3 is still the operative instruction. DFARS Class Deviation 2026-O0025, Revision 3 (signed Sept. 3) directs contracting officers to "remove or revise the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) requirements in new and existing solicitations and contracts" per the DoW CIO's July 13 memo. That memo permits Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self) and requires baseline NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 compliance under DFARS 252.204-7012. Why it mattersUntil the task force reports, this deviation shapes your next contract action, not speculation about reform.

Cyber AB and C3PAOs

  • Most surveyed contractors kept going. Redspin's third annual survey, published October 1 and covered by CMMC.com on October 8, found 78.2% of respondents were either continuing toward Level 2 (C3PAO) certification (46.9%) or already certified (31.3%). The remaining 21.9% had delayed or significantly slowed their work. The survey drew 131 responses in July and August 2026, and seven in 10 respondents work at organizations with fewer than 500 employees. Why it mattersYour competitors are mostly not standing still.
  • Primes are not uniformly relaxing. Per the same write-up, only 23.3% of primes are relaxing Phase 2 expectations for suppliers, 39.5% are still deciding, and 37.2% still require third-party assessments. Meanwhile, 76.6% of subcontractors said they had heard nothing from their prime about the pause. Why it mattersYour prime's flowdown may matter more than the DoW's timeline. Ask rather than assume.

Industry and enforcement

  • No new DOJ cyber-fraud settlements announced Oct 5–9. The most recent we are tracking is Honeywell Aerospace (below). Why it mattersA quiet week doesn't lower False Claims Act risk tied to SPRS scores and 800-171 representations.

Still on the radar

  • Honeywell Aerospace settlement (Sept. 1, 2026). Honeywell Aerospace Inc. agreed to pay $2,042,518 over allegations involving "requirements specified in National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Special Publication (SP) 800-171." DOJ notes the claims are allegations only, with no determination of liability.
  • FY2027 NDAA. The House passed H.R. 8800 on July 22. As of CRS's September 1 update, the Senate had not passed its version (S. 4784). The Senate committee bill would create a CMMC grant program, capped at $50 million and up to $100,000 per recipient, covering direct costs of a Level 2 (C3PAO) assessment for small businesses and new entrants.
  • CAICO renewal window opens November 1. It runs through December 31, per the Cyber AB's September town hall.

Watch list

  • Public release of the task force recommendations.
  • Any Phase 2 restart date, or proposed changes to 32 CFR Part 170 or DFARS 252.204-7021.
  • NDAA final text and whether the CMMC grant program survives.

What to do this week

  • Ask your prime, in writing, what it will require of you in the next 12 months: Level 2 (Self), Level 2 (C3PAO), or SPRS score only.
  • Confirm your SPRS score and annual affirmation still match your actual implementation.
  • Keep closing NIST SP 800-171 gaps. Reform can change the assessment model, not 252.204-7012.
