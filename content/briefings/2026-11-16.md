## Top story

- **The ILC Dover settlement is the largest cyber False Claims Act recovery so far.** On Nov. 3, DOJ announced that ILC Dover LP and ILC Astrospace agreed to pay $13 million to resolve allegations tied to Army, Missile Defense Agency and NASA contracts from 2018 to 2024. Per Washington Technology, the government alleged ILC stored CUI with a cloud provider "without ensuring that provider met FedRAMP Moderate requirements," did not update system security plans, and did not submit current NIST SP 800-171 assessments to DoD. Arnold & Porter (Nov. 10) called it "DOJ's largest single recovery in a cyber FCA matter to date." The relator, ILC's former director of cybersecurity, receives $2.35 million. These are allegations; parent Ingersoll Rand said the settlement was reached "without an admission of liability." *Why it matters:* the pause on Phase 2 did nothing to reduce exposure on what you attest to in SPRS.
- **Correction.** Last week's briefing said no new DOJ cyber-fraud settlements were announced Nov. 2–6. That was wrong: the ILC announcement came Nov. 3 and we missed it.

## Rules and policy

- **Nov. 10 came and went.** It was the original Phase 2 start date for Level 2 (C3PAO) requirements in new contracts. Phase 2 has been suspended since July 13, and no restart date has been announced. A Cyber Sierra FAQ updated Nov. 12 says the task force report, due Sept. 11, "has not been made public." *Why it matters:* there is still no date to plan around, and a restart may come with short notice.
- **Revision 3 still governs contracts.** DFARS Class Deviation `2026-O0025`, Revision 3 (Sept. 3), directs contracting officers to "remove or revise the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) requirements in new and existing solicitations and contracts." We found no Revision 4 last week. *Why it matters:* DFARS `252.204-7012` and NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 still apply, and where a contract still includes a CMMC level, `252.204-7021` still requires a current SPRS self-assessment.

## Cyber AB and C3PAOs

- **No new Cyber AB releases surfaced Nov. 9–13.** The CCP and CCA renewal window, opened Nov. 1, closes Dec. 31, with a grace period through Jan. 30. CCPs and CCAs certified before Jan. 1, 2026 need at least 20 CPEs by Dec. 31. *Why it matters:* if you rely on an in-house CCP or an outside CCA, confirm they renew on time.

## Industry and enforcement

- **Whistleblowers drive these cases.** Arnold & Porter counts 11 of 17 Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative settlements as relator-initiated, many by employees with cybersecurity or IT duties. It also reports the ILC relator's retaliation claim appears to have been resolved for $180,000, and that half of the $13 million was restitution. *Why it matters:* how you handle an internal security complaint can decide whether it becomes a qui tam suit.

## Still on the radar

- **Task force options.** Federal News Network's Oct. 27 report on a September summary listed options, including a narrower Phase 2 restart and 270-day POA&Ms. None has been adopted.
- **FY2027 NDAA.** The House passed H.R. 8800 on July 22; Senate cloture on S. 4784 failed July 14. We found no newer Senate action, so the small-business CMMC grant provision remains pending.

## Watch list

- Release of the task force report or a Phase 2 restart announcement.
- Any Revision 4 to `2026-O0025`, or rulemaking for 270-day POA&Ms.
- The Dec. 31 CCP/CCA renewal deadline.

## What to do this week

- Confirm any cloud service holding CUI is FedRAMP Moderate authorized or has documented equivalency, and keep that evidence with your SSP.
- Check that your SSP is current and your SPRS score is current and matches what you can document.
- Make sure employees have a clear internal channel for security concerns, and that someone owns the response.
